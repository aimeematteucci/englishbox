Can or know?

Real English, real examples. Look at the data, find the pattern, and discover the rule by yourself.

Step 1 · Look at the data 10 min

Concordance lines

These lines come from patterns in real English texts. The key word is in the middle. Look at the words after the key word. Use the buttons to sort the lines.

Show Sort by

The first word after the key word is underlined. On a phone, slide the table to read the full lines.

Step 2 · Sort the lines 8 min

Three groups

Read each sentence. What does it talk about? Tap a group.

Abilitycan / can't + verb
Informationknow + a fact, a person, a place
Learned stepsknow how to + verb

Step 3 · Make the rule 5 min

Your rule

Use what you saw in Steps 1 and 2. Complete the rule.

can + verb
I can swim. Can you drive?
“Eu sei nadar.” Use this for most abilities.
know + information
I know the answer. Do you know where it is?
Saber uma informação, conhecer alguém.
know how to + verb
She knows how to fix computers.
Saber os passos, o método. Less common than can.

Never: I know swim. I know to swim.

In large collections of English texts, people use can + verb for abilities much more often than know how to + verb.

Step 6 · Be the researcher 5 min + homework

Search real English yourself

SkELL is a free search tool for English learners. It shows you real sentences from the internet. Open it in a new tab and follow the steps.

Open SkELL ↗
  1. Type can swim and press Enter. Read five sentences. Who can swim?
  2. Type know how to. Which verbs come after it? Write three.
  3. Type know and click Word sketch. Look at the words after know. Are they verbs?
  4. Write three sentences about you: two with can and one with know.

Your notes stay on this device. Copy them to your notebook or share them with your teacher.
